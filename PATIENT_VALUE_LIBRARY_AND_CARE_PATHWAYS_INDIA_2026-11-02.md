# Patient Value Library & Care Pathway Blueprint — India
**Date:** 2 November 2026  
**Status:** research-backed product proposal for Dr. Kulkarni's review; not a clinical guideline and not yet approved for patient-facing publication.

## Executive direction

Build a patient-and-caregiver knowledge service that helps people live better with illness, recognise deterioration early, use treatment safely, prepare for consultations and navigate recovery at home. Its value is not the number of pages or calculators. It is the quality of practical decisions it helps a patient and family make—without replacing an examination, diagnosis or emergency service.

The patient portal is the primary commercial and service experience. Doctor Workstation is a separate, secondary professional area and must not dominate the first screen. The public journey should be: Home visit / patient help → choose a care need → understand safe next steps → book the appropriate service → retain a useful personal care reference.

## 1. What leading sources teach us

### Mayo Clinic
Mayo's patient education demonstrates how to translate disease management into daily action. Its heart-failure material includes daily weight and swelling checks, personalised salt/fluid advice and green/amber/red action zones; the threshold is an example from its clinical context and must not be copied as a universal Indian rule. Its diabetes material gives practical foot inspection and oral care. Its cirrhosis material explains alcohol avoidance, nutrition/malnutrition, infection prevention, medicine caution and symptom/complication monitoring.  
Sources:
- https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/heart-failure/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20373148
- https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/heart-failure/expert-answers/heart-failure-action-plan/faq-20433731
- https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/diabetes/in-depth/diabetes-management/art-20045803
- https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/cirrhosis/in-depth/cirrhosis-self-care/art-20588994

### Cleveland Clinic
Its Chronic Care Clinic describes a useful service model: educate patients about disease and how to integrate care into life; set goals with patients; monitor progress; adjust plans; support self-management. The clinic explicitly includes CKD, COPD, heart failure and hypertension. This supports a longitudinal “care plan + review” product rather than a static article collection.  
Source: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/departments/chronic-care

### MSD Manual Consumer
MSD's Consumer health topics are organised as an accessible disease encyclopedia across major body systems, medications, older people's health, nutrition and special subjects. Its “When to See a Doctor” resource explains why routine, problem-based and emergency care differ and why a single universal triage list cannot cover every person. This is a useful information architecture and safety lesson, not content to copy.  
Sources:
- https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/health-topics
- https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/special-subjects/making-the-most-of-health-care/when-to-see-a-doctor

### Indian clinical foundation
ICMR's Standard Treatment Workflows cover multiple specialties and specific conditions including diabetes, hypertension, heart failure, CKD, dialysis, stroke, COPD, asthma, cirrhosis/liver failure, cancer and more. MoHFW Clinical Establishments publishes Standard Treatment Guidelines across critical care, cardiovascular, respiratory, gastroenterology, haemodialysis, oncology, endocrinology, neurology and other domains. These are stronger local anchors than simply translating US patient pages.  
Sources:
- https://www.icmr.gov.in/standard-treatment-workflows-stws
- https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/en/standard-treatment-guidelines

### Accreditation and patient education
NABH's patient-centred framework includes access/continuity, care, medication management, patient rights/education and infection control. NABH says patients/families should receive information in a language and manner they understand. JCI similarly emphasises education tailored to assessed patient/family needs and safe transition home. A published patient resource should therefore have a language-access plan, medication reconciliation, teach-back, clear follow-up and documented understanding—not just a disclaimer.  
Sources:
- https://international.nabh.co/standard.aspx
- https://international.nabh.co/whynabh.aspx
- https://www.jointcommissioninternational.org/accreditation/hospital/
- https://www.ahrq.gov/patient-safety/patients-families/engagingfamilies/strategy4/index.html

## 2. High-value patient resources worth building

The following are durable, printable and revisitable tools. They should be free or included with care, with an option to save/print/share. Their value is practical usefulness, not an inflated monetary claim.

| Resource | What the patient keeps | Why it is useful |
|---|---|---|
| My Health Passport | Diagnoses, allergies, baseline function, medicines, devices, emergency contacts, treating team | A compact reference across clinics/hospitals |
| One-page Medication Master List | Drug, strength, purpose, timing, prescriber, start/stop/review date, allergy and side-effect notes | Reduces omissions, duplication and unsafe self-adjustment |
| Personal Red-Flag Action Card | Emergency / same-day / routine thresholds specific to the reviewed condition | Helps decide when not to wait |
| Daily Care Planner | Morning / meals / medicines / movement / rest / evening / sleep | Turns advice into manageable habits |
| Appointment Preparation Sheet | Symptoms timeline, questions, current medicines, reports and goals | Makes consultation more productive |
| Home Monitoring Logbook | BP, glucose, weight, oxygen saturation or symptom entries only when indicated, with context | Turns isolated numbers into interpretable trends |
| Hospital-to-Home Recovery Pack | Discharge medicine reconciliation, wound/device care, mobility, follow-up and red flags | Supports safe transition after discharge |
| Caregiver Handbook | Safe transfers, pressure-area prevention, feeding/swallowing safety, delirium signs, respite | Helps family care without preventable harm |
| Indian Food & Substitution Atlas | Portion visuals, familiar meal patterns, condition-specific substitutions | Practical for Indian homes; avoids foreign menu assumptions |
| Test & Report Explainer | What a test measures, preparation, common reasons for abnormality, limitations and next questions | Improves understanding without diagnosing from one number |
| Preventive Care Calendar | Vaccination, screening and follow-up reminders based on age/risk/sex/condition | Makes prevention actionable |
| Device Skills Library | BP cuff, glucometer, inhaler, spacer, oxygen concentrator, nebuliser, walker, catheter/stoma basics | Demonstration-oriented safety |
| Recovery & Function Tracker | Walking, sleep, appetite, pain, daily activities, mood and patient-defined goals | Values quality of life, not just laboratory numbers |
| Palliative Care Comfort Plan | Symptom diary, goals, caregiver contacts, anticipatory planning and when to call | Supports dignity and comfort alongside disease care |
| Verified Local Care Directory | Emergency departments, labs, imaging, pharmacies, rehabilitation and palliative services with date-checked details | Local navigation; no fabricated endorsements |

## 3. Disease-specific care pathways: what each should contain

Each disease pathway should be an authored, clinician-reviewed mini-handbook with:
1. What the diagnosis means and what it does not mean.
2. What matters to the patient (function, work, family, comfort, goals).
3. Morning-to-night actionable routine.
4. Medicines: exact patient prescription only; adherence, side effects, missed dose, storage, refill and interaction safety.
5. Food/fluid: locally familiar options; restrictions only when justified by diagnosis, labs, organ function and clinician plan.
6. Activity, rehabilitation, sleep, mental health and social life.
7. Home measurements: device, technique, frequency, context and limits.
8. Strictly avoid: condition-specific, evidence-supported hazards and safer alternatives.
9. Emergency / same-day / routine escalation.
10. Family/caregiver tasks and teach-back.
11. Follow-up schedule and what to bring.
12. References, evidence grade, reviewer and review date.

### Priority modules

**Diabetes**
- Hypoglycaemia recognition and action plan; meal/medicine coordination; sick-day rules; insulin storage and injection technique; foot inspection; eye, kidney, dental and cardiovascular prevention; glucose log with meal/medicine context.
- Avoid blanket advice to change insulin/tablets based on a single reading. Medication changes require an authorised clinician plan.
- Indian anchor: ICMR STW Diabetes Type 2 and DKA; Mayo practical foot-care material.

**Hypertension and cardiovascular prevention**
- Validated cuff selection, seated rest, arm position, repeat readings, measurement diary and when readings need clinician review.
- Medication adherence, salt sources in Indian packaged/restaurant foods, tobacco cessation, sleep, activity and risk-factor review.
- No self-directed extra tablet for one high reading unless a specific clinician action plan exists.

**Heart failure / coronary disease**
- Personal weight, oedema, breathlessness and symptom trend plan; medicine reconciliation; activity pacing; vaccination and follow-up; emergency chest-pain/breathlessness plan.
- Salt/fluid targets are individualised; avoid universal litre/day advice or self-adjustment of diuretics.
- Mayo's traffic-light action-plan concept can be adapted only after clinician-defined thresholds.

**COPD / asthma**
- Inhaler technique videos and teach-back; spacer cleaning; trigger and smoke exposure; action plan; exacerbation signs; pulmonary rehabilitation; oxygen fire safety and prescribed-flow-only warning.
- Do not change oxygen flow or use borrowed inhalers without clinical direction.

**CKD / dialysis**
- Stage- and laboratory-specific potassium, phosphate, sodium, protein and fluid plan; dialysis attendance; vascular access care; medication/NSAID safety; vaccination and infection warning signs.
- Never publish a universal “renal diet” or fluid restriction detached from eGFR, potassium, urine output, dialysis modality and clinician/dietitian review.

**Cirrhosis / chronic liver disease**
- Alcohol avoidance, nutrition and sarcopenia prevention, medication/herbal safety, infection prevention, ascites/weight/abdominal girth monitoring if prescribed, encephalopathy and GI-bleed red flags, vaccination and surveillance follow-up.
- Avoid blanket protein restriction; individualise with hepatology/dietitian. New confusion, vomiting blood, black stools, fever with ascites or rapidly worsening jaundice needs urgent assessment.

**Cancer and palliative care**
- Symptom diary (pain, breathlessness, nausea, constipation, appetite, sleep, mood); analgesic safety; bowel plan; nutrition goals; caregiver support; advance-care conversations; when to call oncology/palliative team.
- Respect patient goals and consent. Palliative care can accompany disease-directed treatment; do not equate it with abandonment.

**Older adults / frailty / dementia**
- Falls and home hazard checklist; medication review; nutrition/hydration individualisation; hearing/vision; mobility aids; delirium versus chronic cognitive change; caregiver respite and safeguarding.
- Sudden confusion is not “normal ageing” and requires prompt clinical assessment.

**Post-ICU / hospital discharge**
- Medication reconciliation; oxygen/device/wound/catheter instructions; mobility and nutrition; delirium, sleep and mood; follow-up appointments; warning signs; caregiver competence check.
- Use teach-back: ask the patient/caregiver to explain the plan in their own words.

## 4. Clinical monitoring and calculators: make them decision-support, not diagnosis

Build a clinically governed tool library with:
- BMI (with adult/child distinctions and limitations).
- BP measurement diary and trend graph (not a diagnostic label from one reading).
- Diabetes glucose/HbA1c trend log and hypoglycaemia record.
- eGFR display only from a validated equation with age/sex/creatinine units and explicit limitations; no standalone CKD diagnosis.
- Heart failure daily weight/symptom tracker with clinician-set alert thresholds.
- COPD symptom/exacerbation diary and inhaler-technique checklist; spirometry interpretation reserved for trained clinicians.
- Medication schedule and interaction prompt (source-backed, not exhaustive; pharmacist/doctor review).
- Frailty/falls risk prompts using validated tools only when appropriate.
- Nutrition/weight-loss and pressure-injury risk monitoring with clear scope.
- Palliative symptom scores and patient-reported quality-of-life tracking.

Every calculator needs: validated formula, intended population, exclusions, units, reference source, interpretation bands, “what this cannot tell you”, update/review date, and a clinician escalation route. No invented normal ranges, automatic diagnosis or autonomous medication changes.

## 5. Patient service architecture and safe routing

The proposed commercial tiers can be made clear and ethically separated:

### ₹100 — Online assistant chat
- Clearly disclose whether this is a human assistant or AI-supported workflow.
- Collect minimum necessary information and explicit consent.
- Provide education, appointment preparation, service navigation and red-flag escalation.
- No autonomous diagnosis, prescription, dose changes or emergency management.
- If red flags or uncertainty appear, stop routine chat and route to a clinician/emergency service.

### ₹500 — Doctor consultation by call; video only when clinically necessary
- Identity and location check, consent, chief complaint, duration, severity, red flags, relevant history, allergies, current medicines, vitals if available.
- Doctor decides whether audio is adequate, video is needed, or physical examination/investigations are required.
- Document assessment, limitations of remote consultation, advice, prescription if appropriate, follow-up and safety-net instructions.
- Follow applicable Indian telemedicine rules and professional obligations; do not promise that every condition can be managed remotely.

### ₹1,200 — Home visit
- State geographic coverage, travel/after-hours conditions and what is included.
- Pre-visit triage; emergency exclusions; identity/consent; examination; point-of-care tests only if available and quality controlled; written plan; escalation/transfer when required.
- Clarify that the fee does not guarantee admission, investigations, medicines or emergency transport unless explicitly included.

### Routing logic
IF immediate life-threatening red flag → instruct emergency services/nearest emergency department; do not queue for paid chat.
ELSE IF needs physical examination, unstable vitals, significant deterioration, complex device/wound care or remote uncertainty → clinician triage for home visit / in-person facility.
ELSE IF stable but requires medical assessment → doctor call; video if clinically useful.
ELSE IF education, report explanation, routine preparation or non-urgent navigation → assistant chat with scope disclosure and escalation path.
The decision belongs to the clinician where medical judgement is required. Price must never determine clinical urgency.

## 6. Patient-first website information architecture

1. Home / book a home visit / what help is available.
2. “What is troubling you?” symptom and condition navigation with prominent emergency warning.
3. Health Library: disease guides, daily living, medicine safety, tests, prevention, elderly care, caregiver guides, recovery and palliative care.
4. My Health Folder: downloadable passport, medicine list, reports checklist, monitoring log and appointment sheet. Do not store sensitive health data without explicit consent, security controls and retention policy.
5. Tools: patient-safe calculators and monitoring logs, each with limitations.
6. Services and transparent pricing: ₹100 chat, ₹500 doctor call/video when needed, ₹1,200 home visit, with clinical routing and exclusions.
7. About Dr Kulkarni / qualifications / service area / contact / privacy / grievance route.
8. Doctor Workstation: separate, secondary navigation and access-controlled; never compete with patient booking on the first screen.

## 7. Editorial, clinical governance and legal safety

- Author original explanations; do not copy Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, MSD, ICMR, NABH or JCI copyrighted text, diagrams or branding. Link to sources and summarise in original language.
- Every disease article has a named clinical reviewer, source list, date checked, next review date and version history.
- Distinguish Indian national guidance, international guidance, institutional patient education and expert opinion.
- Marathi and English authored versions need clinical equivalence review. Machine translation is supplementary and must not be presented as a clinician-verified translation.
- A red-flag list must be condition-specific and tested with clinical scenarios.
- Patient education should use plain language, pictograms/video/audio, teach-back and caregiver inclusion with consent.
- Collect the minimum necessary patient data. Keep anonymous educational browsing separate from consultation records.
- No AI system may diagnose, prescribe, change doses, declare a patient safe, or overrule emergency escalation without a legally and clinically governed framework.

## 8. Phased delivery

**Phase A — patient-first foundation:** navigation, full language accessibility, article-level translation, home visit booking, clear pricing, emergency routes, privacy and mobile accessibility.

**Phase B — durable patient assets:** Health Passport, medication list, appointment sheet, BP/glucose/weight logs, discharge pack, caregiver checklist and printable PDFs.

**Phase C — six priority condition guides:** diabetes, hypertension, heart failure, COPD/asthma, CKD/dialysis, cirrhosis. Each receives clinical review before publication.

**Phase D — elderly, cancer/palliative, post-ICU, stroke and other conditions.**

**Phase E — governed assistant and clinician workflow:** consent, structured intake, clinician routing, audit trail, escalation and privacy controls.

## 9. Definition of a genuinely premium patient resource

A patient should be able to save it, print it, show it to another doctor, use it during a difficult day and understand what to do next. It should be accurate, local, short enough to act on, deep enough to trust, reviewed often enough to remain current, and honest about uncertainty. The goal is not to claim a ₹10,000 market value; it is to deliver durable value that a patient would willingly keep and reuse.
