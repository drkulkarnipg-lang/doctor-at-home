This describes the website’s intended editorial process and current limitations. It is not an accreditation, independent clinical audit or legal certification. A draft is not marked medically reviewed unless that review has actually been recorded.

Authorship and responsibility

Identify the author or source of an article where known. Distinguish the website owner from the author and medical reviewer. Imported articles and user-entered bylines are not independently verified. Built-in guides are AI-assisted educational drafts linked to source references; individual clinical sign-off is not currently recorded.

Sources and dates

Prefer original guidelines, government resources, professional societies and primary research. A source-check date describes checking the linked reference; it is not a physician’s review date. Retain publication/update/review dates separately where available. Do not fabricate dates, clinical approval or evidence strength. Research news and RSS excerpts are not equivalent to reading a complete study.

Review and corrections

Check accuracy, scope, units, contraindications, reading clarity and the relevance of the source before publishing individual treatment guidance. Correct substantive errors with a visible explanation and date when a correction is made. The current article editor stores local drafts; it does not yet enforce a multi-reviewer approval system or maintain a complete revision audit log.

Independence and commercial links

Label any advertisement, sponsorship, affiliate arrangement or paid recommendation if introduced; keep it separate from educational content. Do not imply endorsement by Mayo Clinic, MSD Manuals, WebMD, another linked organisation or a drug company. An external link is a reference or service handoff, not a partnership or certification. The site must not claim independent verification of a clinician’s expertise or a product’s superiority without evidence.

AI and translation

AI can help draft or organise material; it does not replace clinical responsibility. Automatic translations may alter medical meaning. English is the source language and clinical documents are excluded from automatic page translation. Do not send patient identifiers to external AI or search tools without an appropriate authorised workflow.

Services and clinical limits

Describe only services currently available. A visit request or fee estimate is not a confirmed slot or an emergency-response guarantee. Education and calculators support discussion; they do not provide an individual diagnosis or prescription. Certificates require the appropriate assessment, verified details and signature, and official forms must meet the receiving authority’s requirements.

Access, privacy and accessibility

Keep patient information separate from public education. Display the actual local/cloud connection state and do not present a browser privacy screen as secure server authentication. See the legal and privacy information for storage and sharing limitations.

Use readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, meaningful headings, descriptive controls, clear units and consistent navigation. Page titles and breadcrumbs identify the current section. These improvements do not constitute a completed WCAG conformance audit; mobile, translated-page and assistive-technology checks remain necessary.
