Meaningful everyday care.

For older adults, people recovering from illness and the families beside them. Build the daily plan with your healthcare team.

Protect skin & comfort

  • Agree an individual repositioning and pressure-relief plan.
  • Check skin, moisture and comfort; report persistent redness or broken skin.
  • Use the support surface and wound-care plan advised for the patient.
Pressure-injury guidance ↗

Support stroke recovery

  • Follow the swallowing assessment and prescribed food textures.
  • Support an upright, comfortable position for meals.
  • Stop feeding and seek help for choking, new breathlessness or reduced alertness.
Swallowing information ↗

Live well with kidney disease

  • Keep dialysis, medicine and blood-test appointments.
  • Ask for your individual fluid, salt and potassium plan.
  • Changes to dialysis frequency belong with the nephrology team.
Kidney-care information ↗

Make home safer

  • Keep walkways clear and lighting comfortable at night.
  • Review safe footwear, mobility aids and bathroom supports.
  • Report new confusion or a fall; the cause needs assessment.

Food & daily living

Familiar food.
An individual plan.

Keep the foods and traditions you know. Adapt portions, textures and nutrients to the person’s care plan.

01 / HEART HEALTH

Make room for less salt.

Discuss everyday meals such as bhakri, dal and vegetables with your clinician or dietitian. Compare packaged-food labels and reduce added salt, pickles and salty snacks.

Fluid limits and weight-alert thresholds should follow the individual heart-failure action plan.

02 / KIDNEY HEALTH

Let the blood tests guide you.

Protein, potassium, phosphorus and fluid needs differ with CKD stage and dialysis treatment. Coconut water, fruit juices and other potassium-rich foods need individual review, not a universal ban.

Use preparation methods such as vegetable leaching only when the renal dietitian advises them.

03 / SWALLOWING

Comfort begins with safe textures.

Soft foods such as porridge may suit some people, but texture and drink thickness must follow a swallowing assessment.

Support upright eating and the recommended pace. Do not force food or fluid when swallowing is unsafe.
